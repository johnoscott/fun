Sputnik 1 — Orbit & Ground Track

The world's first artificial satellite, launched 4 October 1957. It looped the Earth every ~96 minutes on a plane tilted 65° to the equator, while the planet turned beneath it. Left: the orbit on the spherical Earth as seen from space — every pass woven together. Right: the same track flattened onto a map.

Because the orbit was inclined 65°, the track reaches 65°N and 65°S — carrying Sputnik over most of the inhabited world. Drawing all ~1,440 orbits it completed before reentry produces the interlaced "spirograph" pattern on the globe: the orbital plane stays essentially fixed among the stars while the Earth rotates through it, so consecutive ground tracks fan out around the planet and gradually fill the band between the two 65° limits. Sputnik beeped for about three weeks until its batteries died, then decayed from atmospheric drag and burned up on reentry on 4 January 1958.
